# Does the form differ by city?

Script: `src/cityforms.py`. The mirror of [14-object-forms.md](14-object-forms.md):
city as the variable, medium as the control. It also settles the question that
one left open — Harappa supplied 387 of 470 tablets, so "tablet convention" and
"Harappa convention" were confounded.

## C1 — the answer is neither. It is an interaction.

| | n | ends in 400 | ends in 740 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mohenjo-daro seal | 928 | 4% | 40% |
| Harappa seal | 254 | 4% | 37% |
| Mohenjo-daro tablet | 83 | 7% | 39% |
| **Harappa tablet** | **387** | **30%** | 24% |

- tablets, MD vs Harappa: 6/83 vs 118/387 — **p = 3.7e-06**
- seals, MD vs Harappa: 36/928 vs 9/254 — **p = 1.00**
- Harappa, seal vs tablet: 9/254 vs 118/387 — **p = 1.1e-19**

Three of the four cells behave identically and one does not. Ending a text with
400 is **not** a tablet habit and **not** a Harappa habit. It is a Harappa
*tablet* habit — one medium, in one city.

## C2 — the same interaction in length

| | Mohenjo-daro | Harappa | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| seal | 4.85 | 4.73 | .21 |
| tablet | 4.39 | **3.51** | **8e-05** |

Seals are the same length everywhere. Harappa's tablets are the short ones.

## C3 — the seal form is uniform across the civilisation

Terminal-slot fillers, seals only:

| | Mohenjo-daro (n=928) | Harappa (n=254) |
|---|---|---|
| 740 | 40% | 37% |
| 520 | 9% | 9% |
| 390 | 5% | 4% |

**chi2 p = 0.98.** Two cities 600 km apart, over a thousand seals, and the
distribution of what ends a seal text is indistinguishable. On tablets the same
test gives p = 1.1e-07.

This is the most striking single number in the analysis. The seal formula is
standardised across the Indus world; the variation is confined to tablets.

## C4 — the numeral gap was medium, not geography

| | Mohenjo-daro | Harappa | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| seal | 58.8% | 61.0% | .56 |
| tablet | 43.4% | 35.1% | .17 |

Neither comparison is significant. The seal/tablet numeral gap found in
[14-object-forms.md](14-object-forms.md) is entirely a property of the medium and
carries no regional component.

## C5 — almost no sign picks a city

64 signs testable, **3** city-specific at |z| > 3, and the strongest is 400
(z = −3.8) — which is the Harappa-tablet effect leaking through. Compare
[14-object-forms.md](14-object-forms.md), where 7 signs were medium-specific and
400 hit z = −8.6.

**Medium sorts the corpus roughly twice as strongly as geography does.**

## C6 — the smaller towns all look like Mohenjo-daro

| site | n | ends 740 | ends 400 | similarity to MD | to Harappa | leans |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dholavira | 74 | 30% | 1% | 0.928 | 0.834 | Mohenjo-daro |
| Lothal | 64 | 23% | 0% | 0.913 | 0.798 | Mohenjo-daro |
| Chanhujo-daro | 50 | 32% | 0% | 0.891 | 0.830 | Mohenjo-daro |
| Kalibangan | 49 | 27% | 4% | 0.924 | 0.851 | Mohenjo-daro |

All four lean Mohenjo-daro on sign-frequency cosine, and **none of them use the
400 ending** (0–4%). The Harappa tablet convention did not travel. Note these
sites are mostly seals, so part of this is the medium effect again.

## Verdict

**The form does not vary by city — it varies by medium, plus one local
exception.**

- the seal template is the same at Mohenjo-daro and Harappa (**p = 0.98**) and
  the minor towns follow it
- the one real regional practice in the corpus is **Harappa tablets**: shorter,
  and ending in 400 thirty percent of the time against four percent everywhere
  else
- medium separates the corpus about twice as strongly as geography

For the record reading this cuts both ways. A standardised document format
maintained across a civilisation is a strong administrative signature. But the
hoped-for finding — many local variants of one template, the fingerprint of
local practice — is not there. There is one template, one deviant medium at one
site, and otherwise remarkable uniformity.

That uniformity is itself the finding, and it is consistent with what is known
archaeologically about Indus standardisation in weights, bricks and town
planning.
